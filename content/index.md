<h1 style="font-family: Courier New">What is this? What's a solo-rpg?</h1>
<hr>
<p style="font-family: Courier New">
For those unfamiliar, you can think of solo tabletop roleplaying games like making your own single-player video game in your head. You pick a genre, a system of rules and game mechanics that fit said genre, and then use that system to help create your setting, character(s), and story.<br>
To use my campaign as an example, I wanted to play in a cyberpunk science-fantasy. Think Cyberpunk 2077 with aliens and magic. So, I chose Ironsworn: Starforged as my system with a community hack called Cybersworn to facilitate this!
<br><br>
Since this is an rpg actual play, it's important to note this is written a bit differently than a novel or other forms of text-based fiction. At certain points, you'll see inserts stating a mechanical action a character takes and the outcome, or maybe a loose description of traits for a character I just rolled up on a series of tables to introduce in the next scene. You don't need to know the Ironsworn system to understand these inserts, though!
<br><br>
Additionally, I won't always give thorough descriptions of things for a handful of reasons.<br>
 - From a narrative and worldbuilding perspective, I enjoy leaving some things open to get filled in later, and the Ironsworn system heavily encourages that. So, the plot and setting are kinda unfolding and revealing itself to me at the same time it is to you!<br>
 - For physical character descriptions, the core cast and significant NPCs will have visual and written references with their respective profiles in the <a href="https://aevuslabyrinth.github.io/Solo-Actual-Play/setting/characters/">Characters</a> section.<br>
 - And, as this is ultimately a game that I'm playing for me, if I don't feel like writing out an explanation for something in a session, I'm just not going to. If it's something of importance, I'll at least make sure it's detailed in the Setting section.
</p>
